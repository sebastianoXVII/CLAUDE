# Retail trading concepts and technical analysis: empirical evidence, and documented retail day-trader performance

Research note, compiled 2026-11-06. Method: web search only. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (papers.ssrn.com, statoasis.com and indicatoredge.io did not resolve), so findings come from abstracts, bibliographic records (RePEc/IDEAS, EconBiz), author/institution PDFs listed in search results, and secondary summaries. Each item is marked by evidence tier:
- **[R]** rigorous: peer-reviewed, or a working paper with an explicit data-snooping or statistical design
- **[I]** independent quantitative backtest, not peer-reviewed
- **[V]** vendor, practitioner or anecdotal source

---

## 1. Classic TA evidence (BLL 1992, STW 1999, LMW 2000, Park & Irwin 2007, Bajgrowicz & Scaillet 2012, Marshall-Cahan-Cahan 2008, Hsu-Taylor-Wang 2016)

### Takeaway
Simple daily rules (moving averages, trading-range breaks) looked profitable in old US equity data (pre-~1990). Once data-snooping is controlled, out-of-sample persistence is tested and costs are included, the edge disappears for liquid equity indices. That includes intraday rules on SPY. FX is the main partial exception, mostly in emerging-market currencies, and it fades over time. Nothing in this literature supports chart-reading edges on modern liquid index futures.

### Cited Findings
- **[R] Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999, JF 54:1647–1691)** extended the 26 rules of Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992) to a universe of about 7,846 rules on 100 years of daily DJIA data. They used White's Reality Check bootstrap to measure data-snooping bias. Later summaries report that no rule was significantly profitable for the DJIA, the S&P 500 or S&P 500 futures in the more recent subsample once snooping was controlled — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:54:y:1999:i:5:p:1647-1691); [PDF](https://bashtage.github.io/kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/Sullivan_Timmermann_White.pdf); [Re-examination with SPA test](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/211/Re-Examining%20the%20Profitability%20of%20Technical%20Analysis%20with%20Whitets%20Reality%20Check%20and%20Hansen9s%20SPA%20Tes.pdf)
- **[R] Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992, JF)** is the original 26-rule study (MA and trading-range-break rules) on the DJIA, 1897–1986. It reported that buy signals were followed by higher returns than sell signals. This is the base set STW extended — [STW paper, which describes BLL](https://bashtage.github.io/kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/Sullivan_Timmermann_White.pdf)
- **[R] Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000, JF 55:1705–1765)** automated pattern recognition (head-and-shoulders, double bottoms and others) with nonparametric kernel regression on US stocks, 1962–1996. They concluded that "several technical indicators do provide incremental information and may have some practical value." The finding is distributional (conditional return distributions differ). It is not a net-of-cost trading profit — [MIT page](https://web.mit.edu/Alo/www/Papers/techanal.html); [NBER w7613](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7613)
- **[R] Park & Irwin (2007, J. Economic Surveys 21(4):786–826)** found that modern studies "indicate that technical trading strategies consistently generate economic profits in a variety of speculative markets at least until the early 1990s." Most studies, however, suffer from "data snooping, ex post selection of trading rules or search technologies, and difficulties in estimation of risk and transaction costs" — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jecsur/v21y2007i4p786-826.html); [AgMAS 2004 report](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/37487/files/AgMAS04_04.pdf). The commonly quoted tally (about 56 of 95 modern studies positive) could not be verified from the abstract.
- **[R] Bajgrowicz & Scaillet (2012, JFE 106(3):473–491)** retested the STW universe on the DJIA, 1897–2011, with a false-discovery-rate approach. Persistence tests showed that "an investor would never have been able to select ex ante the future best-performing rules." Even in-sample, "the performance is completely offset by the introduction of low transaction costs" — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:106:y:2012:i:3:p:473-491); [PDF](https://bashtage.github.io/kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/Bajgrowicz_Scaillet.pdf). A secondary summary says backtested outperformance vanishes across the rule universe for 1997–2007 — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1631)
- **[R] Marshall, Cahan & Cahan (2008, J. Empirical Finance 15(2):199–210), "Does intraday technical analysis in the U.S. equity market have value?"** Using bootstrap methods, they found that none of the 7,846 popular rules produced significant returns on 5-minute SPDR (SPY) data. A secondary summary gives the sample as 2002–2003 and notes the results are before costs, so costs would only make them worse — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v15y2008i2p199-210.html); [CXO summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/technical-analysis-anathema-to-the-academic-world)
- **[R] Hsu, Taylor & Wang (2016, J. International Economics 102:188–208), "Technical trading: Is it still beating the foreign exchange market?"** The study covers more than 21,000 rules, 30 developed and emerging currencies and about 45 years of daily data, using the step-SPA test (Hsu, Hsu & Kuan 2010), which controls data snooping, plus out-of-sample cross-validation. It finds "substantial predictability and excess profitability" in FX. Summaries say the effectiveness decays over time and survives mainly in emerging-market currencies — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:inecon:v:102:y:2016:i:c:p:188-208); [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/16362/); [CXO summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/updated-comprehensive-long-term-test-of-technical-currency-trading)

### Inferences
- The pattern is the same across studies. Edges show up in old, less efficient or less liquid markets and are not robust to multiple-testing correction, out-of-sample selection or realistic costs. For intraday ES/NQ, Marshall et al. (SPY, 5-min, no edge even before costs) is the closest rigorous analogue, and it is negative.
- LMW (2000) is the strongest academic support for chart patterns carrying information. "Information" (different conditional distributions) is not the same as tradable net profit.

### Gaps
- No STW/Bajgrowicz-style data-snooping-corrected study on intraday E-mini (ES/NQ) futures with modern (post-2015) data was found in this search.
- The Park & Irwin study count (56/95) and the exact BLL statistics were not verified from primary text.

## 2. Support/resistance, round numbers, stop-loss clustering, "liquidity sweeps"

### Takeaway
This is the one retail concept with a real microstructure basis. Stop-loss and take-profit orders demonstrably cluster at and just beyond round numbers (Osler, FX dealer data). Executed stops produce self-reinforcing price cascades. Chart-derived S/R levels coincide with depth peaks in the limit order book (Kavajecz & Odders-White). Round-number clustering creates buy/sell imbalances (Bhattacharya, Holden & Jacobsen). What is not established is that "liquidity sweeps" are intentional hunts, or that a rule like "trade the reversal after a level is swept" earns money net of costs in index futures. False-breakout statistics circulating online have no verifiable methodology.

### Cited Findings
- **[R] Osler (2003, JF; NY Fed Staff Report 150), "Currency orders and exchange-rate dynamics: explaining the success of technical analysis."** Using a large FX dealing bank's order book, the study finds that take-profit orders cluster particularly strongly at round numbers, while stop-loss orders cluster strongly just beyond them. Executed sell stops sit just below round numbers and buy stops just above. This explains two TA claims: trends tend to reverse at S/R (take-profit clusters) and to accelerate after S/R is broken (stop clusters) — [NY Fed SR150 PDF](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf); [JF record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/1540-6261.00588)
- **[R] Osler (2005, "Stop-loss orders and price cascades in currency markets")** finds that stop-loss orders are sometimes triggered in waves, contributing to price cascades. This may help explain fat tails in FX returns. The response to stop-loss triggers lasts longer than the response to take-profit triggers, and most effects are statistically significant for hours but not days. Osler cautions that the statistics cannot prove causality — [Conference version](https://icmaif.soc.uoc.gr/Year/8conf/docs/Osler-Price%20Cascades-03-03.doc); [Fed in Print](https://fedinprint.org/item/fednsr/12305). A secondary blog reports her figures that orders ending in exactly 00 made up 9.9% of take-profit value and 3.8% of stop-loss value, against 1% under a uniform distribution — [Tradeciety](https://tradeciety.com/the-order-clustering-effect-around-round-numbers) (secondary; not checked against the original)
- **[R] Osler (2000, FRBNY Economic Policy Review, "Support for resistance: technical analysis and intraday exchange rates")** is the earlier paper showing that S/R levels published by FX firms had some predictive power for intraday trend interruptions. This is from training knowledge; the paper was not retrieved in this session (expected location: newyorkfed.org EPR 2000 vol. 6 no. 2). Treat it as unverified here.
- **[R] Kavajecz & Odders-White (2004, RFS 17(4):1043–1071), "Technical analysis and liquidity provision."** Support and resistance levels coincide with peaks in depth on the limit order book, and moving-average signals carry information about where depth sits relative to the quote. The relationships stem from technical rules locating depth already in place, so TA "can have value even in an efficient market" as a liquidity-mapping tool. The authors do not claim it predicts returns — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v17y2004i4p1043-1071.html). A later Taiwan study with reconstructed order books reports similar results — [SUFE newsletter](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/355597)
- **[R] Bhattacharya, Holden & Jacobsen (2012, Management Science 58(2):413–431), "Penny Wise, Dollar Foolish."** This study documents buy-sell imbalances on and around round numbers, through cluster undercutting of limit orders at round prices and "threshold trigger" effects — [PDF](https://host.kelley.iu.edu/cholden/Bhattacharya%20Holden%20and%20Jacobsen%20(2012).pdf)
- **[R] Bourghelle & Cellier (Euronext)** find that clustering of limit orders at round numbers creates salient points where latent liquidity accumulates. This is consistent with strategic placement of orders just beyond round numbers — [EFMA 2007 paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2007-Austria/papers/0286.pdf). A J. Financial Research (2009) study on limit-order clustering finds that "price clustering indeed creates price barriers" — [IDEAS (Financial Review 2009)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/finrev/v44y2009i1p1-29.html) (venue as reported by the search summary)
- **[R] Donaldson & Kim (1993)** found that round-number (100-point) levels of the DJIA (1974–1990) acted as support/resistance. A study of Asian indices found barriers in Korea and Taiwan, mild ones in Japan and Hong Kong, and weak ones in Singapore and China — [core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/286099507.pdf) (as summarized by search)
- **[R/arXiv] Chung & Bellotti (2021), "Evidence and Behaviour of Support and Resistance Levels in Financial Time Series"** is a statistical investigation of S/R levels as temporary price barriers. The paper notes that both sides of the S/R debate have "equally numerous and convincing academic evidence" — [arXiv 2101.07410](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2101.07410); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2101.07410.html). From training knowledge, not verified this session: it reports that prices bounce off S/R more often than chance and that bounce probability rises with prior touches. Verify before citing numbers.
- **[V] False-breakout statistics.** Figures such as "50–70% of intraday breakouts fail" and "about 58% on EURUSD with 7,100+ breaks" appear on retail sites with no published methodology — [ForTraders](https://fortraders.com/blog/false-breakouts-why-they-happen-how-to-trade). Bookmap's guidance on stop runs (aggressive volume without follow-through) is qualitative heuristics — [Bookmap](https://bookmap.com/blog/stop-hunt-or-just-noise-how-to-read-the-real-intent-behind-a-move). Practitioner sources concede that sweeps follow "naturally from how liquidity is distributed — no assumption of manipulation is required" — [BullSmart](https://blog.bullsmart.in/fake-breakouts-in-nifty-stop-loss-options-future/)
- **[V]** Stop-loss locations and sizes are not publicly available on any exchange. Retail "stop cascade" indicators therefore model stop locations rather than observe them — [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/CJX3k6l2-stop-loss-cascades-breakouts/)

### Inferences (mechanisms and how to operationalize)
- **Plausible mechanism.** Stops cluster just beyond salient levels: round numbers, prior highs and lows, and plausibly session highs and lows such as the Asian or London range and prior-day high/low. When price reaches the cluster, the stops become market orders and give a short-lived impulse (a cascade). Take-profit and limit clusters at the level itself produce bounces. Both "sweep and reverse" and "break and run" can therefore occur. The microstructure prediction is about conditional volatility and order-flow asymmetry, not a directional edge in either case.
- **Testable rules for index futures:**
  - (a) Define levels mechanically: prior-day high/low, overnight/Globex high/low, 00/50 handles in ES, 000/500 in NQ.
  - (b) Event: price trades beyond the level by k ticks. Classify a "sweep" as a close back inside within N bars, otherwise a "breakout".
  - (c) Measure forward returns, the realized-volatility jump and the fill rate at 1, 5, 15 and 60 minutes, conditional on the event, against randomly placed placebo levels with the same distance distribution.
  - (d) Control for the snooping over k, N and level types with White's RC or Hansen SPA/step-SPA. Use walk-forward selection and include realistic costs (ES: 1 tick slippage plus commissions).
- Osler's data is FX, 1999–2000, from one dealer. Transferring it to CME futures with modern HFT liquidity provision is an untested assumption.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that measures false-breakout or sweep-reversal frequencies at prior highs/lows in ES/NQ against a placebo baseline.
- No study was found on stop clustering in CME equity-index futures order books. Exchange stop data is not public.

## 3. ICT / Smart Money Concepts (FVG, IFVG, order blocks, killzones, Silver Bullet, Power of 3/AMD, MSS/BOS/CHoCH), pre-session/Asian/London ranges

### Takeaway
ICT/SMC is a retail-education framework originating with Michael J. Huddleston ("The Inner Circle Trader"). No peer-reviewed study supports it. The only academic-style systematic evaluation found is a July 2026 SSRN working paper by Shepherd Mahadzva on G10 FX. Its finding is negative: no evidence that the concepts work "as literally taught". The independent quantitative backtests that exist (StatOasis, indicatoredge) are also negative or at best mixed. Nothing on arXiv was found. Session-range concepts (Asian/London range) have no academic tests either. The closest academic relative is opening-range-breakout work on US equities (Zarattini & Aziz), whose gross results reportedly do not survive costs in independent replication.

### Cited Findings
- **[R-ish, working paper, not peer-reviewed] Mahadzva (SSRN, July 2026), "Smart Money or Costly Folklore? A Systematic Evaluation of ICT/Smart-Money-Concepts Trading Rules in G10 FX."** According to the abstract excerpts, the author finds no evidence that ICT/SMC concepts work as literally taught. In one case a strategy's gross edge was smaller than the fixed per-lot commission. A bug in an order-block detector went undetected by the project's own statistical checks, which is a methodological warning about how fragile SMC rule implementations are. Per a search summary, the FVG was among the concepts not tested by its rule set — [SSRN 7430998](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7430998). Full text, sample period and test statistics could not be retrieved because SSRN was blocked in this session.
- **[I] StatOasis, "I Backtested ICT / Smart Money Concepts — What Survives."** This codifies FVG, order blocks, liquidity sweeps and OTE (optimal trade entry) into mechanical rules on daily bars for SPY and three other markets. None of the four core concepts showed a statistically significant forward-return edge. Order-block and FVG entries beat simple textbook entries on SPY, and FVG "managed a majority on two of four markets" — [StatOasis](https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives) (from search snippets; page not fetched). It is not intraday and not on futures.
- **[I] indicatoredge.io, "Does Smart Money Concepts Actually Work? Backtested"** tested SMC rules on 738 assets. None of the concepts reliably beat buy-and-hold. The FVG row shows an average alpha of -7.7% and beat buy-and-hold in only 13% of assets — [indicatoredge](https://indicatoredge.io/smart-money) (from snippets). Buy-and-hold is a weak benchmark for intraday or short strategies, and costs and timeframe are unknown.
- **[V] Vendor claims** (Backtrex, fxnx and others) of FVG win rates above 60% come without disclosed methodology — [Backtrex FVG](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/fair-value-gap-trading-strategy); [fxnx](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/smart-money-concepts-work-backtest-evidence). Build Alpha publishes a how-to for codifying ICT/SMC, not results — [Build Alpha](https://www.buildalpha.com/backtest-ict-and-smc/)
- **[V] Open-source code.** GitHub hosts vectorized SMC backtesters (liquidity sweep plus FVG entries, vectorbt) with self-reported results and no multiple-testing control — [frankabingale1111/smc-backtest](https://github.com/frankabingale1111/smc-backtest); [GitHub topic ict-concepts](https://github.com/topics/ict-concepts). MQL5 articles implement OB/BOS/FVG detection as EAs — [MQL5 16340](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/16340). A Feb 2026 Reddit post describes a rule-based order-block backtest across markets with no methodology — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qvovpv/snapshots/2026-02-06T06%3A00%3A05.034107Z). None of these reaches the [I] standard.
- **arXiv:** a search for "smart money concepts", "order blocks" and "fair value gap" returned no arXiv papers. Results were TradingView scripts, broker blogs and forums — e.g. [LuxAlgo SMC family](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/family/smc-ict.md)
- **Origin and guru verification:** no CFTC enforcement action or court filing against Huddleston was found. One forum post alleges an investigation with no source — [ForexPeaceArmy forum](https://www.forexpeacearmy.com/community/goto/post?id=257337). This should be treated as unsubstantiated. No independently audited track record was found.
- **[V] Gap/imbalance fill rates (closest FVG relative).** No peer-reviewed intraday gap-fill or FVG-fill study was found. A vendor reports a 6-month sample of ES gap-ups filling about 64% and gap-downs about 63% at a full-fill target, NQ at about 51% and 55%, rising sharply at a 50% target (ES gap-downs 63% to 87%) — [Edgeful](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/half-gaps-vs-full-gaps). NexusFi and a closed-source TradingView script give unsourced fill probabilities by ATR bucket — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/fill-the-gap); [TradingView script](https://www.tradingview.com/script/32pGGSWx-Gap-Fill-Probability-Statistics-ES-NQ). A high "fill rate" alone is not an edge. Retracement into a nearby range is the base-rate behaviour of a random walk, and it must be compared with a placebo and include stop-outs.
- **[R/I] Opening-range breakout (closest academic relative of session-range concepts).** Zarattini & Aziz (2023, SSRN 4416622) report a 5-minute ORB on QQQ with 33.2% CAGR against 14.9% buy-and-hold (Jan 2016–Feb 2023, 1,795 trades, zero slippage assumed) — [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy). The follow-up by Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (2024, SFI RP 24-98) applies the idea to "Stocks in Play" — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html). An independent replication on five indices: "gross reproduced, net zero" — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235) ([I], not peer-reviewed).
- No academic study of the Asian-range or London-range breakout/sweep was found.

### Inferences (plausible mechanisms and how to operationalize)
- **Liquidity sweep / stop hunt / AMD "manipulation" leg.** This is the only ICT element with a documented mechanism: Osler-style stop clustering beyond highs and lows (section 2). The ICT framing of deliberate "algorithmic" manipulation by an "interbank price delivery algorithm" has no evidential support. The mechanical (non-intentional) version can be tested as in section 2.
- **Killzones / Silver Bullet (time windows).** The real basis is the well-documented intraday seasonality of volume and volatility: the US cash open, the London open and macro-release times. Volatility concentrated in a window raises the variance of outcomes, not the expected return. Test: does the strategy's net expectancy inside the window beat the same rules at random times with matched volatility?
- **FVG (three-candle gap, where the wick of candle 1 does not overlap the wick of candle 3).** Plausible reading: a fast one-sided move leaves thin liquidity at the intermediate prices, and a later retracement into it is common simply because price mean-reverts over short horizons. Operationalize as follows:
  - FVG defined in ticks, with a minimum size as a multiple of ATR, on 1, 5 and 15-minute bars.
  - Entry at the gap midpoint (consequent encroachment) on first touch, stop beyond the gap, target R multiples.
  - Compare with placebo "gaps" placed at random prices of the same distance.
  - The inverse FVG is the same object with direction flipped after a close through the gap. Testing both directions doubles the snooping burden.
- **Order blocks** (the last opposite candle before a displacement) and **MSS/BOS/CHoCH** (swing-high/low breaks defined with fractal or pivot lengths): these depend on discretionary parameters (pivot length, displacement threshold). Mahadzva's undetected order-block bug shows that implementation choices drive the results. Any test must fix definitions ex ante and correct for the whole grid of parameter combinations (SPA/step-SPA, FDR as in Bajgrowicz & Scaillet).
- **Asian/London/pre-session range.** Testable as "range high/low swept and reclaimed during the NY session leads to reversal toward the opposite side of the range" against a "breakout continues" alternative, for ES/NQ with session times in exchange time. Use the ORB literature as a template and include costs, which in the Zarattini replication erased the edge.
- **Round numbers in ES/NQ** (00 and 50 handles, 000/500 in NQ): the microstructure basis is the strongest of all these concepts (sections 2 and 4). Operationalize as in section 2.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or thesis-level test of ICT/SMC on index futures (ES/NQ) was found for 2020–2026. The only systematic paper is on FX, its detailed results could not be read, and it is a single unrefereed working paper.
- The StatOasis and indicatoredge methodologies (costs, timeframe, period, multiple-testing control) could not be read because fetches were blocked.
- No study was found on Power of 3/AMD, Silver Bullet, killzones or IFVG specifically.
- No QuantConnect public study of ICT/SMC with credible design was surfaced.

## 4. Retail day-trader performance and skill persistence

### Takeaway
The large-sample administrative evidence is overwhelmingly negative.
- **Taiwan:** less than 1% of day traders predictably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees, and aggregate day-trader performance is negative in every year of the 15 studied.
- **Brazil, mini-Ibovespa futures** (the closest analogue to retail index-futures day trading): 97% of those who persisted more than 300 days lost money, and about 1% earned more than the minimum wage.
- **EU regulators:** 74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose money.

Skill does persist among a tiny top tier (top 500 Taiwanese day traders), so the claim "nobody has skill" is false. The claim "a retail trader can expect to learn to be profitable" is unsupported.

### Cited Findings
- **[R] Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (2014, J. Financial Markets 18:1–24), "The cross-section of speculator skill: Evidence from day trading"** covers all Taiwan Stock Exchange day traders, 1992–2006. Ranking traders by prior-year returns shows persistence. The top 500 earn 61.3 bps/day before fees and 37.9 after, while the bottom group earns -11.5 before and -28.9 after. "Less than 1% of the population of day traders is able to predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees" — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24); [Working paper PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- **[R] Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean (and Zhang), "Learning, fast or slow" (day trading and learning)** finds that aggregate day-trader performance net of fees is negative in each of the 15 years studied. Most quit after losses, and few learn — [Working paper PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)
- **[R] Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (2009, RFS 22(2):609–632), "Just how much do individual investors lose by trading?"** Individual investors' trading losses equal about 2.2% of Taiwan's GDP, or 2.8% of personal income, and the annual performance penalty for individuals is 3.8 percentage points. Nearly all individual losses trace to aggressive (market-taking) orders. Institutions gain about 1.5 pp a year, with foreign institutions taking close to half of institutional profits. Slightly under two-thirds of individual losses are transaction costs — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:22:y:2009:i:2:p:609-632); [RFS PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/justhowmuchdoindividualinvestorslose_rfs_2009.pdf); [Berkeley Haas news](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/?p=10491)
- **[R] Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti (2019/2020, FGV/USP working paper), "Day Trading for a Living?"** follows 19,646 individuals (per a summary) who began day trading mini-Ibovespa futures in 2013–2015, using regulator (CVM) data. 97% of those who persisted more than 300 days lost money. Only 1.1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage, and only 0.5% (0.4% in another version) earned more than a bank teller's starting salary (about US$54/day). The authors find no evidence of learning and conclude it is "virtually impossible for individuals to compete with HFTs and day trade for a living, contrary to what course providers claim" — [SSRN 3423101 via RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fgv/eesptd/525.html); [FGV repository](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b/full); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/). Caveat: the 97% applies to persisters (300+ days), not to all entrants.
- **[R/regulatory] ESMA (March 2018) product intervention under MiFIR Art. 40.** National competent authorities' analyses showed that 74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose money, with average losses per client of about €1,600 to €29,000. ESMA banned binary options for retail clients and restricted CFDs (leverage caps, margin close-out, negative balance protection, an incentive ban, and a mandatory risk warning stating the provider's percentage of losing accounts) — [ESMA press release](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933); [RPC Legal](https://rpclegal.com/thinking/financial-services-regulatory-and-risk/esma-cracks-down-on-the-sale-of-binary-options-and-cfds-to-retail-investors); [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/?p=348240). Brokers still cite the 74–89% range in risk disclosures — [Exante](https://exante.eu/retail_risk_warning/)

### Inferences
- The Brazilian futures result is the most relevant one for intraday index-futures day trading, and it is the most negative. Combined with the BLLO finding that losses come from aggressive orders and costs, the main structural headwind for a retail intraday trader is the cost of crossing the spread plus commissions against HFT liquidity providers. This is why gross backtest edges (Zarattini ORB, Mahadzva's commission case) disappear net.
- Skill persistence exists, but only in the extreme tail (top 500 traders in all of Taiwan, under 1%). Survivorship-filtered success stories are therefore expected even when the population mean is strongly negative.

### Gaps
- No comparable administrative study of US retail E-mini/micro E-mini day traders was found. CFTC/CME do not publish retail P&L distributions.
- Current (2024–2026) ESMA loss-rate updates were not retrieved, and neither were post-2020 retail-boom studies (for example of options traders), which were outside the searches done.

## 5. Narrative and marketing issues: guru survivorship and unverifiable P&L

### Takeaway
The marketing of retail concepts relies on discretionary, after-the-fact chart annotation and self-reported P&L. With hundreds of thousands of participants and a less-than-1% skilled tail, visible "successful" educators are exactly what survivorship would produce even with no edge in the taught method. Course sellers' incentives are tied to selling courses, not to trading results. Chague et al. explicitly frame their findings against course providers' claims.

### Cited Findings
- Chague et al. conclude, contrary to course providers' claims, that day trading for a living is virtually impossible — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fgv/eesptd/525.html)
- Less than 1% of day traders show persistent net-of-fee skill — [EconPapers BLLO 2014](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)
- No independently audited track record for the ICT originator was found. The only "investigation" claim is an unsourced forum post — [ForexPeaceArmy](https://www.forexpeacearmy.com/community/goto/post?id=257337)
- Vendor backtest claims (FVG win rates above 60%) have no disclosed methodology — [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/fair-value-gap-trading-strategy). Mahadzva's paper shows that even a systematic implementation can hide detector bugs that statistical checks miss — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7430998)
- Data snooping and ex post rule selection are the main methodological failures in the TA literature (Park & Irwin) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jecsur/v21y2007i4p786-826.html). For ICT/SMC, which has many discretionary parameters, the effective number of rules tested is very large.

### Inferences
- Minimum standard for evaluating any concept in the downstream report:
  - mechanical definition fixed ex ante
  - placebo or random-level baseline
  - multiple-testing correction across the parameter grid (RC, SPA/step-SPA or FDR)
  - walk-forward or out-of-sample periods
  - realistic tick slippage and commissions
  - reporting of the full distribution, not just win rate
- Win rate is a misleading marketing metric. Stop-and-target geometry can produce any win rate with zero expectancy.

### Gaps
- No academic study was found that quantifies survivorship bias or misreporting specifically among trading educators or influencers. This search covered none, and the claim rests on inference from population statistics.
- The Michael Huddleston biography (start of public teaching, 2010s YouTube/"2022 mentorship") was not verified from a primary source in this session.
