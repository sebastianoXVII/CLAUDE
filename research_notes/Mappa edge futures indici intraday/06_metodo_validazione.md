## 1. Multiple testing, data snooping and backtest overfitting

### Takeaway
A backtest found by searching over many variants has to be judged against the best result that pure luck would produce across that many trials. The main tools are White's Reality Check and Hansen's SPA (is the best rule better than a benchmark?), Romano-Wolf stepdown (which rules are better, with familywise error control), the Harvey-Liu-Zhu t > 3 hurdle and the Harvey-Liu haircut, the Deflated Sharpe Ratio, and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting (CSCV). For a retail or prop intraday developer, the most practical setup is (a) log every variant tried, (b) compute DSR with N equal to the number of effectively independent trials, and (c) compute PBO across the parameter grid.

### Cited Findings
- **White (2000), "A Reality Check for Data Snooping", Econometrica 68(5).** Tests whether the best of many candidate models beats a benchmark, given that the winner was picked using the same data. The bootstrap is taken over the whole set of models. Weakness: low power when the set includes many poor or irrelevant alternatives. — [nm.dev on White's Reality Check](https://nm.dev/2011/07/28/on-whites-2000-reality-check/); [MetricGate RC/SPA doc](https://metricgate.com/docs/reality-check-superior-predictive-ability/)
- **Hansen (2005), "A Test for Superior Predictive Ability", JBES 23, 365–380.** Studentizes each model's performance and drops clearly inferior models from the recentering step. The result is more power and less sensitivity to irrelevant alternatives than the Reality Check. — [Hansen 2005 (typeset)](https://typeset.io/pdf/a-test-for-superior-predictive-ability-1fbgz05jht.pdf); [Brown WP 2003-9](https://economics.brown.edu/sites/g/files/dprerj726/files/papers/2003-9_paper.pdf)
- Hansen and Lunde applied both tests to 330 GARCH-type models and found that the Reality Check lacked the power to separate good models from bad ones. — [Hansen & Lunde (typeset)](https://typeset.io/pdf/a-forecast-comparison-of-volatility-models-does-anything-2omhzb6x2m.pdf)
- **Romano & Wolf (2005), "Stepwise Multiple Testing as Formalized Data Snooping", Econometrica 73(4), 1237–1282**, DOI 10.1111/j.1468-0262.2005.00615.x. A stepwise procedure that asymptotically controls the familywise error rate. It rejects more false nulls than single-step methods because it captures the joint dependence of the test statistics. The authors recommend studentization. — [Econometrica page](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/browse/2005/07/01/stepwise-multiple-testing-formalized-data-snooping); [UZH PDF](https://uzh.ch/cmsssl/econ/dam/jcr:ffffffff-935a-b0d6-ffff-ffffa286d4d1/etca.pdf)
- **Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016), "…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", RFS 29(1), 5–68** (NBER WP 20592, 2014). A new factor needs t > 3.0 rather than 2.0, given how much data mining the field has done. They conclude that most claimed findings in financial economics are likely false. At least 315 factors had been tested. — [NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592); [SSRN 2513152](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2513152); [RePEc RFS](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:29:y:2016:i:1:p:5-68.)
- **Harvey & Liu (2015), "Backtesting", JPM 41(1), 13–28.** Gives a method to haircut any reported Sharpe ratio for multiple testing, plus a profit hurdle. The authors call the usual flat 50% haircut "a serious mistake" because the true haircut is nonlinear: high Sharpe ratios are penalized moderately and marginal ones heavily. Inputs are the significance level, the method (Bonferroni, Holm, BHY, or their average) and the number of tests. — [Duke PDF P120](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF); [CME-hosted copy](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/education/files/backtesting.pdf); [quantstrat profitHurdle / haircut implementation](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/quantstrat/versions/0.16.7/topics/profitHurdle)
- Secondary report (not verified against the paper): an SR of 0.75 haircut to about 0.32 (about 60%) under the paper's assumptions. — [Advisor Perspectives](https://api.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2016/04/28/special-edition-can-you-trust-investment-research)
- **Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado 2012, "The Sharpe Ratio Efficient Frontier", Journal of Risk 15(2)).** PSR is the probability that the true SR exceeds a threshold SR\* when returns are non-normal. It also defines the Minimum Track Record Length. — [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/journal-risk/2223785/sharpe-ratio-efficient-frontier); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/rsk/journ4/2223785.html); [PerformanceAnalytics MinTrackRecord](https://rdrr.io/cran/PerformanceAnalytics/man/MinTrackRecord.html)
  - PSR(SR\*) = Φ[ (SR̂ − SR\*)·√(n−1) / √(1 − γ₃·SR̂ + ((γ₄−1)/4)·SR̂²) ], with γ₃ = skewness, γ₄ = (non-excess) kurtosis, and SR measured at the sampling frequency (per trade or per day, not annualized). — [López de Prado slides (QWAFAFEW)](https://newyork.qwafafew.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2017/01/Lopez_de_Prado_Sharpe.pdf); units caveat: [quantt DSR note](https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/deflated-sharpe-ratio)
  - MinTRL = 1 + [1 − γ₃·SR̂ + ((γ₄−1)/4)·SR̂²] · (z_α / (SR̂ − SR\*))². This is the standard form implemented in PerformanceAnalytics; it was not re-verified against the paper text. — [PerformanceAnalytics MinTrackRecord](https://rdrr.io/cran/PerformanceAnalytics/man/MinTrackRecord.html)
- **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado 2014), JPM 40(5), 94–107.** Corrects for selection bias under multiple testing and for non-normal returns. DSR = PSR with SR\* raised to the expected maximum SR across N zero-skill trials: SR₀ = √V[SR_n] · [(1−γ_E)·Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/N) + γ_E·Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/(N·e))], where γ_E ≈ 0.5772 (Euler–Mascheroni) and V[SR_n] is the variance of the SRs across trials. — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); [Wikipedia: Deflated Sharpe ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflated_Sharpe_ratio); expected-max formula as given by [MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/ja/deflated-johnson-sharpe-ratio/) (secondary); [quantstrat deflatedSharpe](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/quantstrat/versions/0.16.7/topics/deflatedSharpe)
- **Probability of Backtest Overfitting (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu), Journal of Computational Finance 20(4), DOI 10.21314/JCF.2016.322** (SSRN 2013/forthcoming 2015, print 2016/17). PBO is estimated with combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV). The authors argue that standard regression hold-out methods are unreliable for investment backtests. CSCV gave reasonable PBO estimates with small errors in their examples. — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [Risk.net JCF](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-computational-finance/2471206/the-probability-of-backtest-overfitting); [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf); [WMU record](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/42)

### Inferences
- **CSCV recipe (from the paper's design):** build a T×N matrix of per-period P&L for N strategy configurations. Split the rows into S equal blocks (S even, e.g. 16), and form all C(S, S/2) train/test combinations (12,870 for S=16). In each combination, pick the configuration with the best IS metric, find its relative rank ω̄ in OOS, and compute the logit λ = ln(ω̄/(1−ω̄)). PBO = share of combinations with λ ≤ 0, i.e. the IS winner ranks below the OOS median. Rule of thumb: reject the family if PBO > roughly 0.2–0.5.
- **Practical DSR for intraday futures:** N should be the number of effectively independent trials, not the raw grid size. Neighbouring parameter sets are highly correlated, so clustering the variants' return series (López de Prado's approach) gives a more honest N than either 1 or the full grid.
- For a single pre-registered intraday rule with daily SR measured on about 250+ days, the HLZ hurdle t > 3 is roughly equivalent to annualized SR ≥ 3/√years, i.e. about 1.34 over 5 years. This follows from t ≈ SR_ann·√years, which ignores non-normality.
- Use Romano-Wolf or SPA when several setups are selected together (for example ORB, VWAP reversion and gap fill across ES, NQ and RTY). Use DSR/PBO for parameter tuning of one setup.

## 2. Cross-validation, labeling, walk-forward, hold-out and sample size

### Takeaway
Time-series leakage through overlapping labels and serial correlation breaks ordinary k-fold. López de Prado's purged/embargoed k-fold and combinatorial purged CV (CPCV) address it, and CPCV yields a distribution of OOS paths instead of one. Walk-forward analysis (Pardo) is the traditional alternative, with "walk-forward efficiency" above about 50–60% as a common rule of thumb. Required sample size grows with 1/SR², so modest per-trade edges need hundreds to thousands of trades.

### Cited Findings
- AFML (López de Prado 2018, Wiley, ISBN 978-1-119-48208-6) contents: Ch. 3 covers the triple-barrier method (§3.4) and meta-labeling (§3.6). Ch. 7 covers purged k-fold CV (§7.4), purging the training set (§7.4.1) and embargo (§7.4.2). The backtesting chapters cover the Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation method. — [Table of contents PDF (uni.li)](https://toc.uni.li/FLMF049641.pdf); [Wiley/Lehmanns listing](https://www.lehmanns.de/isbn/978-1-119-48208-6)
- Financial labels are built over overlapping windows, so plain k-fold leaks information. Purging removes training observations whose label windows overlap the test set. An embargo removes observations right after the test set to block leakage through serial correlation. — [TradingInterview lesson on purging/embargo](https://www.tradinginterview.com/courses/machine-learning/lessons/cross-validation-for-financial-data-purging-and-embargoing/)
- Triple barrier: labels come from whichever is hit first among a profit-take barrier, a stop-loss barrier and a time expiry, rather than from fixed-horizon returns. Meta-labeling: a secondary model decides whether to act on, and how to size, a primary model's signal. — [replication note (non-authoritative)](https://qgtm-docs.pages.dev/research/gold_silver/paper_lopez_de_prado_afml); [AFML TOC](https://toc.uni.li/FLMF049641.pdf)
- Pardo, *The Evaluation and Optimization of Trading Strategies* (2nd ed., Wiley 2008, ISBN 978-0-470-12801-5). Ch. 11 "Walk-Forward Analysis" includes "Robustness and Walk-Forward Efficiency" (p. 238). — [Wiley-VCH listing](https://www.wiley-vch.de/en/areas-interest/finance-economics-law/the-evaluation-and-optimization-of-trading-strategies-978-0-470-12801-5); [typeset TOC](https://typeset.io/pdf/the-evaluation-and-optimization-of-trading-strategies-1v4948xmy2.pdf)
- WFE = annualized OOS performance / annualized IS performance. A vendor attributes to Pardo a minimum WFE of more than 50%, and others cite more than 50–60% as the rule of thumb for "not over-optimized". Implementations differ on the metric used. — [Backtrex WFE](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/walk-forward-efficiency-ratio-thresholds); [ProRealCode](https://www.prorealcode.com/blog/learning/strategy-optimisation-walk-analysis/); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/walk-forward-analysis.md)
- **Stationary bootstrap (Politis & Romano 1994), JASA 89(428), 1303–1313, DOI 10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870.** Resamples blocks of random length drawn from a geometric distribution, so the resampled series stays stationary. Used for standard errors and confidence regions with weakly dependent data. — [UCY Gnosis record](https://gnosis.library.ucy.ac.cy/handle/7/57533); [MathWorks implementation](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/101929-stationary-bootstrap/)

### Inferences (formulas; standard statistics)
- **Sample size for expectancy significance.** With per-trade mean μ and std s, t = (μ/s)·√N. To reach t ≥ z you need **N ≥ (z·s/μ)² = (z/SR_trade)²**. Examples: SR_trade = 0.10 (typical of a decent intraday setup) needs N ≈ 400 trades for z = 2 and about 900 for z = 3 (HLZ). SR_trade = 0.05 needs 1,600 and 3,600.
- **Win-rate test.** For a fixed-payoff system with reward/risk R, the breakeven win rate is p₀ = 1/(1+R) after costs. To show p > p₀ you need N ≥ z²·p(1−p)/(p−p₀)². Example: R = 1, p = 0.55, p₀ = 0.50 (ignoring costs) gives N ≈ 396 at z = 2.
- **Autocorrelation.** With lag-k autocorrelations ρ_k of trade or daily returns, the effective sample size is N_eff ≈ N / (1 + 2Σρ_k). Alternatively, use Newey-West standard errors or the stationary bootstrap. Intraday trades on the same day, or on correlated index futures (ES/NQ), are not independent, so aggregate to daily P&L before testing.
- **Bootstrap CI for expectancy.** Resample daily P&L blocks with the stationary bootstrap (mean block length roughly 5–20 days, chosen by Politis-White automatic selection), recompute mean, PF and max DD, and take the 2.5% and 97.5% percentiles. An iid trade bootstrap understates tail risk when losses cluster.
- **Hold-out discipline.** Write down the hypothesis, universe, session, entry/exit logic, cost model and the list of variants before testing. Keep the final 20–30% of the data, ideally including a different volatility regime such as 2022 bear or 2020 crash vs 2023–25, untouched and touch it once. Every peek at the hold-out adds to N for DSR.
- **Regime dependence.** Report results split by volatility tercile (VIX or realized vol), trend vs range years, and pre/post major microstructure changes (e.g. the 2019 Micro E-mini launch, 0DTE options growth from 2022). An edge present in only one regime calls for a regime filter, which counts as another tested hypothesis.

## 3. Cost modeling, fill simulation, look-ahead and data handling

### Takeaway
For intraday index futures, costs and fill assumptions often decide whether an edge exists at all. Market orders should pay at least half the spread plus impact (1 tick round-trip minimum on ES/NQ during RTH, more at the open, at news and overnight). Limit orders should not be filled "on touch". Require a trade-through, or model queue position from L2/L3 data. Bar-based backtests must resolve intrabar stop/target ambiguity conservatively and avoid look-ahead. Continuous contracts must be back-adjusted to suit the calculation.

### Cited Findings
- Queue-aware fill simulation (hftbacktest) models fills using queue position plus feed and order latency, and reconstructs the full book from L2 market-by-price and L3 market-by-order data. Its docs say finding the right queue-position model by comparing backtest with live results is "essential", and they compare square, log and power probability queue models. — [hftbacktest Probability Queue Models](https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/Probability%20Queue%20Models.html); [PyPI hftbacktest](https://pypi.org/project/hftbacktest)
- Continuous contracts: additive (Panama) back-adjustment keeps price differences (tick P&L) correct but can make old prices negative and distorts percentage returns. Ratio adjustment keeps percentage returns but loses absolute levels. Use unadjusted data for ratio or level-dependent logic. — [QuantStart continuous futures](https://www.quantstart.com/articles/Continuous-Futures-Contracts-for-Backtesting-Purposes); [NexusFi back-adjusted](https://nexusfi.com/a/data/continuous-contracts-back-adjusted-data); [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/rolling-on-the-futures-curve-continuous-futures-prices-99382ee4bb4e/); [QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/continuous-futures-contracts-explained)

### Inferences (practitioner checklist; no single primary source)
- **Market or stop orders:** slippage ≥ 1 tick per side on ES/NQ during RTH, plus a volatility-scaled component (e.g. k·ATR_1min). Stress-test at 2× slippage. Stop orders in fast markets slip more than entries.
- **Limit orders:** "fill on touch" is optimistic and suffers adverse selection: you get filled when price goes through you and miss when it bounces. Conservative rule: fill only if price trades at least 1 tick through the limit. Better: estimate queue position from the displayed size at order arrival and require traded volume at that price to exceed it.
- **Intrabar ambiguity:** when one OHLC bar contains both stop and target, assume the stop was hit first, or re-simulate on 1-second or tick data. Never use the bar's close or high to decide an entry that executes inside that same bar.
- **Look-ahead:** compute signals on completed bars only. Watch session VWAP/high/low that include the current bar, daily indicators that use today's close in intraday logic, and "first hour" ranges referenced before the hour has ended. Time stamps should be bar-end and time-zone consistent (CME Globex ET/CT, DST).
- **Sessions and rollover:** define RTH vs ETH explicitly. Roll on volume crossover, roughly 8 days before expiry for equity index quarterlies. Intraday strategies should trade the actual front contract, with prior-day references (PDH/PDL, settlement) taken from the same contract.
- **Commissions and fees:** include exchange, NFA and clearing fees plus the platform fee per side. For micros, fixed fees are a large share of the tick value.

## 4. Performance metrics and their significance

### Takeaway
Report per-trade and per-day statistics together: expectancy with a t-test and bootstrap CI, PF, payoff, win rate, Sharpe (with PSR/DSR), Sortino, max DD, MAR/Calmar and Ulcer Index. Per-day aggregation is the right unit for prop-rule modeling because daily loss limits and consistency rules are daily.

### Cited Findings
- Sharpe values in PSR and DSR must use the native sampling frequency, not annualized values. — [quantt DSR](https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/deflated-sharpe-ratio)
- PSR works like the p-value of the test H₀: SR ≤ SR\*. — [Portfolio Optimizer blog (indexed snippet)](https://portfoliooptimizer.io/blog/the-probabilistic-sharpe-ratio-bias-adjustment-confidence-intervals-hypothesis-testing-and-minimum-track-record-length/)

### Inferences (standard definitions)
- Expectancy E = p·W̄ − (1−p)·L̄. Payoff ratio = W̄/L̄. Profit factor PF = ΣWins/|ΣLosses| = p·W̄/((1−p)·L̄). E > 0 ⇔ PF > 1.
- Significance: t = Ē/(s/√N) on net trade P&L. Better: on daily P&L, with Newey-West or stationary bootstrap standard errors.
- Sharpe SE (iid, approximately normal): SE(SR) ≈ √((1 + SR²/2)/T). This is Lo (2002), adjusted for skew and kurtosis in the PSR denominator.
- Sortino = mean / downside deviation. MAR (Calmar) = CAGR / max DD. Ulcer Index = √(mean(DD%_t²)), where DD%_t is the percentage drawdown from the running peak. It penalizes depth and duration together.
- Max DD scales roughly with σ·√T for zero-drift paths, so a single historical max DD is one draw. Use the Monte Carlo/bootstrap distribution, e.g. the 95th-percentile DD.

### Gaps
- Ulcer Index (Martin & McCann 1989) and Lo (2002, FAJ "The Statistics of Sharpe Ratios") primary URLs were not retrieved in this session.

## 5. Prop-firm challenge modeling: pass probability, trailing drawdown, sizing

### Takeaway
A challenge is a two-barrier first-passage problem: reach +T (target) before hitting the loss floor. With a static floor at −D, the Brownian-with-drift closed form applies. With a **trailing** floor (drawdown D from the running peak), the classic Taylor/Lehoczky drawdown result shows that pass probability falls sharply. At zero edge it goes from D/(T+D) to e^(−T/D). Intraday trailing (based on the unrealized peak) is stricter than EOD trailing. Daily loss limits, consistency rules, minimum days and time limits require Monte Carlo on your own bootstrapped daily or trade P&L.

### Cited Findings
- Two-barrier hitting probability for Brownian motion with drift μ and volatility σ, starting at 0, upper barrier U, lower barrier −L: **P(hit U first) = (1 − e^(−2μL/σ²)) / (1 − e^(−2μ(U+L)/σ²))**. Worked example: μ=0.5, σ=1, U=2, L=1 gives ≈ 0.665. — [QuantVault problem 416](https://quantvault.org/problem-416-first-passage-probabilities-and-optimal-barriers-for-brownia.html)
- One-sided limit: with positive drift, P(ever reach −a) = e^(−2μa/σ²). — [QuantVault problem 3677](https://quantvault.org/problem-3677-brownian-motion-with-unit-drift-probability-of-ever-reaching.html)
- Practitioner sources frame the challenge as gambler's ruin. They note that trailing drawdown with asymmetric payoffs has no clean closed form, so tools simulate the full rule set (target, floor, daily loss limit, minimum days) by resampling the trader's own trades. Pass-rate heuristics differ widely between tools ("> 65% comfortable", "> 70% good"), and one source claims an industry pass rate of about 10%. None of these are independently verified. — [DEV Community Monte Carlo for prop firms](https://dev.to/propfirmkey/monte-carlo-simulation-for-prop-firm-risk-assessment-1ckb); [CrossTrade Monte Carlo](https://crosstrade.io/learn/performance-metrics/monte-carlo-simulation); [NexusFi EV of evaluations](https://nexusfi.com/a/prop-firms/expected-value-prop-firm-evaluations); [NexusFi sizing](https://nexusfi.com/a/prop-firms/position-sizing-funded-evaluations)
- EV of an evaluation: EV = p·μ − F (p = pass probability, μ = expected net payout given a pass, F = all-in fees including resets and activation). — [NexusFi EV](https://nexusfi.com/a/prop-firms/expected-value-prop-firm-evaluations)
- Claim (unverified): trailing drawdown cuts pass rates by 30–40% compared with static. — summarized in search results from vendor/blog sources ([CrossTrade trailing DD guide](https://crosstrade.io/learn/risk-management/trailing-drawdown-survival-guide))
- EOD trailing recalculates the floor once at session close from the highest EOD balance, so intraday pullbacks don't count. Intraday trailing follows the highest unrealized equity, including open-trade peaks. — [TradingFunder EOD vs intratrade](https://tradingfunder.com/concepts/eod-vs-intratrade-drawdown/); [Topstep vs Apex (Topstep blog)](https://topstep.com/blog/topstep-vs-apex)
- Firm rules conflict between sources and change often. Topstep's blog describes its funded accounts as EOD and Apex as intraday trailing. Third parties say Apex offers a choice of EOD or intraday at purchase. Consistency thresholds are reported variously as 30%, 40% or 50% depending on firm, product and date. Verify against the firms' current help centers. — [Topstep blog](https://topstep.com/blog/topstep-vs-apex); [TradeTanto Apex rules 2026](https://tradetanto.com/learn/apex-trader-funding-rules-what-you-need-to-know); [TradingFunder Topstep vs Apex](https://tradingfunder.com/compare/topstep-vs-apex/); [Futureshive DD & consistency calculator](https://www.futureshive.com/tools/prop-firm-drawdown-calculator)
- **Sizing under drawdown constraints.** Grossman & Zhou (1993, Mathematical Finance 3(3), 241–276) maximize growth subject to wealth staying above a fraction of its running maximum; this is the continuous-time trailing-floor problem. Busseti, Ryu & Boyd (2016, Journal of Investing; arXiv 1603.06183) add a bound on drawdown probability to Kelly as a convex program. In simulations it beats fractional Kelly at equal drawdown risk. A later study found that the Grossman-Zhou policy can produce negative expected returns near the floor once discrete trading and costs are included. — [Boyd: Risk-Constrained Kelly Gambling](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/kelly.html); [PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/kelly.pdf); [IDEAS arXiv 1603.06183](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/1603.06183.html); [Cvitanić–Karatzas drawdown paper (typeset)](https://typeset.io/pdf/on-portfolio-optimization-under-drawdown-constraints-36ds75vqlb.pdf); [QuantInsti summary](https://blog.quantinsti.com/risk-constrained-kelly-criterion/)

### Inferences (derived formulas; check by simulation)
- **Static floor.** Define γ = 2μ/σ², with μ and σ² the per-day (or per-trade) mean and variance of P&L in $. Then P_pass = (1 − e^(−γD)) / (1 − e^(−γ(T+D))). At zero edge P_pass = D/(T+D). Example: a $50K account with T = $3,000 and D = $2,000 gives 40% at zero edge.
- **Trailing floor (drawdown D from running peak; Taylor 1975 / Lehoczky 1977 maximum-drawdown result).** P(running max reaches T before a drawdown of D) = exp(−T·γ/(e^(γD) − 1)). As μ→0 this tends to exp(−T/D). Example: T = 3,000, D = 2,000 gives e^(−1.5) ≈ 22% at zero edge, against 40% static. Many firms stop trailing once the floor reaches the starting balance (or start + $100). That makes the account static from then on, so simulate the switch explicitly. The formula is from the author's knowledge of Lehoczky, "Formulas for stopped diffusion processes with stopping times based on the maximum", Ann. Probab. 5(4), 1977 (https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-probability/volume-5/issue-4/Formulas-for-Stopped-Diffusion-Processes-with-Stopping-Times-Based-on/10.1214/aop/1176995770.full). It was not fetched in this session.
- **Effect of size.** Trading k contracts scales drift by k and variance by k², so γ_k = 2μ/(kσ²) while T and D stay fixed in $. With **positive edge**, smaller size gives a higher pass probability but slower progress, which conflicts with time limits and fees per month. With **zero or negative edge** after costs, static-floor pass probability is independent of size at μ = 0 and *increases* with size when μ < 0. That is the "bold play" result of Dubins–Savage, and it explains why aggressive sizing "works" for traders without an edge yet produces negative EV after fees.
- **Kelly vs ruin.** Full Kelly (f\* = μ/σ² per unit of capital) implies roughly a 1−x chance of ever drawing down to fraction x of peak. A prop account with D ≈ 4% of nominal makes full Kelly on nominal capital lethal. The relevant "bankroll" is D, not the nominal account. Size so that the probability of a drawdown of D before reaching T is acceptable, typically risk per trade ≈ D/10 to D/20, then verify by Monte Carlo.
- **Daily loss limit (DLL).** Treat it as a per-day absorbing barrier on intraday P&L paths. It truncates bad days but also ends days that would have recovered. It requires intraday path data (trade sequence within day), not just daily totals.
- **Intraday vs EOD trailing.** Under intraday trailing, the floor rises with the open-trade MFE. The effective drawdown is then measured from peaks that include unrealized excursions, so a strategy with large MFE that gives profit back (wide targets, trailing exits) is punished heavily. EOD trailing only sees closes. Simulate with trade-level MFE/MAE.
- **Consistency rule** (best day ≤ x% of total profit). This acts as an extra constraint on the path. Simulate by rejecting or extending paths until the condition holds, which raises expected days to pass and fee exposure.
- **Monte Carlo procedure.** (1) Get net-of-cost trade list with timestamps, MFE and MAE. (2) Block-bootstrap by day (stationary bootstrap) to keep within-day dependence. (3) Apply the exact firm rules (target, trailing type, DLL, min days, max days, consistency, contract cap, news restrictions). (4) Record pass rate, days to pass, and the funded-phase survival curve P(survive n payouts). (5) Compute EV = p_pass·E[payouts | funded] − E[fees]. (6) Repeat under a degraded edge (e.g. 50% of backtest expectancy, per Section 6) to get a realistic band.
- **Funded survival.** Same barrier model with T replaced by payout thresholds and buffer rules. Survival through k payout cycles is roughly the product of per-cycle survival probabilities if cycles are independent, which they are not when the floor locks at the start balance.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or academic paper was found specifically on prop-firm challenge pass-probability math. The literature consists of vendor blogs and simulators.
- Official current rules for Topstep, Apex and others could not be fetched (topstep.com was blocked). The sources conflict and need direct verification.
- Industry-wide pass and payout rates are not credibly published. Figures in circulation (~10% pass, "0.71% make it through") come from unverified third-party sites.

## 6. Live vs backtest degradation evidence

### Takeaway
The largest public study, Quantopian's 888 algorithms, found that the backtest Sharpe ratio has almost no predictive power for out-of-sample Sharpe. The more backtests an author ran, the bigger the IS–OOS gap. Risk measures (volatility, drawdown) persisted better than returns. Plan on substantial haircuts: start at 50% or more of backtest expectancy, nonlinear as in Harvey-Liu.

### Cited Findings
- Wiecki, Campbell, Lent & Stauth (Quantopian, 2016), "All that Glitters Is Not Gold: Comparing Backtest and Out-of-Sample Performance on a Large Cohort of Trading Algorithms" (SSRN 2745220). 888 algorithms were filtered from 7,152, backtested 2010–2015, each with at least 6–12 months of true OOS. Commonly reported backtest metrics, including Sharpe, were poor predictors of OOS performance. ML on behavioral features did predict OOS performance to some extent, and a portfolio selected that way beat one built from the highest backtest Sharpe ratios. — [Paper PDF (mql5 mirror)](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/208/All%20that%20Glitters%20Is%20Not%20Gold%20-%20Comparing%20Backtest%20and%20Out-of-Sample%20Performance%20on%20a%20Large%20Cohort%20o.pdf); [Quantopian forum archive](https://quantopian-archive.netlify.app/forum/threads/q-paper-all-that-glitters-is-not-gold-comparing-backtest-and-out-of-sample-performance-on-a-large-cohort-of-trading-algorithms); [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-888-trading-strategies); [HedgeFundAlpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/comparing-backtest-sample-performance-large-cohort-trading-algorithms/)
- Reported magnitude: IS performance explains only about 1–2% of OOS behaviour across individual metrics (R² < 0.025 for Sharpe). The R² figure appears in secondary summaries and in the author's recollection of the paper; verify in the PDF. — [Quantpedia summary (indexed)](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)
- Supporting evidence that backtest-selected Sharpe is inflated: the HLZ "most findings likely false" conclusion and the Harvey-Liu nonlinear haircut (Section 1). — [NBER w20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592); [Duke P120](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)

### Inferences
- From the author's recollection of the paper, not verified here: the number of backtests run was positively associated with the IS–OOS Sharpe shortfall, and volatility and max drawdown were more persistent than Sharpe or returns. Practically, trust backtest risk estimates more than return estimates, and size prop accounts from the backtest drawdown distribution while assuming expectancy decays.
- Combined decision rule for an intraday futures edge before risking evaluation fees: (1) a pre-registered hypothesis, (2) t > 3 on daily net P&L or DSR > 0.95 with honest N, (3) PBO < 0.2 on the parameter grid, (4) WFE > 50% and a positive untouched hold-out, (5) survives 2× slippage and trade-through fills, (6) Monte Carlo pass probability with 50% expectancy decay still gives EV > fees.

### Gaps
- The mql5 PDF and Quantpedia pages were not fetched in full (quantpedia.com blocked), so the exact regression coefficients from Wiecki et al. are unverified.
- No public dataset of live-vs-backtest results specific to retail or prop intraday futures traders was found.
